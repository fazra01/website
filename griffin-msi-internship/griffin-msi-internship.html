Confidential Exhibit Design Renderings - Griffin MSI Chicago

Pre-announcement project — exhibit name to be added after public release, 2025
During Summer 2025, I worked at the Kenneth C. Griffin Museum of Science and Industry in Chicago as a Design Intern. I built 3D environment models and produced weekly renderings for a confidential exhibit. These renders guided spatial layout, lighting, and artifact protection discussions for leadership reviews. Details and cleared images will be added after the museum’s official announcement.

Design Process

I joined the design team midway through the exhibit’s conceptual development phase. My responsibilities centered on translating physical measurements into digital models, generating iterative renders, and refining lighting schemes that aligned with the curatorial narrative. I participated in cross-department meetings where creative direction, accessibility, and artifact presentation strategies were discussed. Weekly feedback sessions shaped how I approached realism, scale, and the emotional tone of light.

Render Studies

A selection of elevation and overview renders used during weekly internal design reviews to compare layout, lighting, and spatial rhythm across rooms.

Outcomes & Reflection

Outcome: Renderings used in design reviews Feedback: Invitation to return next summer
Impact: Faster iteration cycles Focus: Human-factors integration
Precision is storytelling. Watching panels go up and wayfinding tuned taught me how much visitor experience lives in small details. In 3D, I treated every render as a question rather than an answer. Weekly passes let us test layouts and lighting before any physical build—balancing playfulness with rigor.
